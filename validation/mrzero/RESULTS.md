# Cross-validation: blochparty vs. MRzero

Generated by `validation/compare_mrzero.py` (MRzeroCore 1.1.1, pypulseq 1.5.0.post1). 32×32 Shepp-Logan (3 T tissue table), ±20 Hz B0 ramp, 4 birdcage coils; every sequence written once with pypulseq and read by both simulators.

## Point isochromats

MRzero runs point-like (voxel dephasing function 1, no Nyquist cut-off) with pruning thresholds at 1e-12 and one pre-pass per tissue; blochparty reads with `convention="mrzero"`. What the remaining differences are:
- **MRzero's numerics, not physics.** Against the exact reference blochparty is at 1e-9–1e-7 (6e-7 on bSSFP, below), MRzero at 1e-6–4e-4. Running MRzero's import and main pass in float64 (column 4; a test-only patch, `_mrzero_float64`) removes most of it on the GRE and spin echo, not on bSSFP: there ~4e-6 remains, about half of it off-resonance during the pulses (below); with 0.02 µs pulses 1–2e-6 is left, which we have not traced further. What is left on the RF-spoiled GRE comes from MRzero's pre-pass merging PDG states whose k-vectors agree only to float32 precision (|k| reaches ~4000 /m after 40 TRs of spoiling, where float32 resolves ~2e-4 /m) and then using the first ancestor's k: up to ~1e-4 rad of phase at the edge of the FOV.
- **Finite pulses.** MRzero applies RF as instantaneous rotations at the pulse centre, so all pulses are 0.2 µs hard pulses. Off-resonance during them (±20 Hz) is a ~1e-6 effect (EPI: 1e-7 without B0); blochparty's second-order Strang splitting of relaxation during RF gives the 6e-7 against the exact reference on bSSFP (4e-8 with `subdivide_rf(4)`).
- **Pruning.** With MRzero's defaults (200 states, magnitude 1e-4, emitted/latent signal 1e-2), the point-isochromat GRE and bSSFP lose most of their signal: those thresholds assume realistic voxels, where high-order states dephase. Here every threshold is 1e-12. MRzero's default pre-pass also uses the phantom's *mean* T1/T2 and prunes the long-lived CSF states (T2 = 2 s) as decayed; on the spin echo that alone was a 6e-5 difference (1e-4 NRMSE), so the pre-pass runs once per tissue (`compute_graph(..., tissues=...)`).
- **Same file, same numbers.** Pulseq rounds amplitudes when writing, so a phase-encode rewinder built separately does not cancel its prephaser exactly. That is the same in both simulators, but it splits PDG states MRzero then merges; rewinders are built with `scale_grad(prephaser, -1)`.
- **Conventions.** MRzero rotates by the Pulseq RF phase as written, tips +z to −y (it rotates the other way from blochparty) and multiplies the signal by exp(i(π/2 − ADC phase)); with `convention="mrzero"` that is the constant −i, fitted above to 1e-5 or better. blochparty's default ("physical") reading conjugates RF and ADC phases so that a frequency offset excites the slice at +f/G; `tests/sequences/test_pulseq.py` checks that the two readings are exact mirror images. MRzero ignores ADC frequency offsets, so none are used here.
- **Ordering.** Both return samples in time order; the trajectory column shows MRzero's own k-space at its samples equals pypulseq's to its float32 precision.

## Voxel model: MRzero box voxels vs. jittered isochromats

MRzero's box voxel (each state's signal weighted by sinc(k·Δx), the exact integral over a uniform square voxel; no Nyquist cut-off) against blochparty with K×K stratified-jittered isochromats per voxel (one uniform random point per sub-cell).

| sequence | box vs. point voxels in MRzero | 1×1 | 2×2 | 4×4 | 8×8 | 16×16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| single-shot EPI | 7% | 1.2e-02 / 6.7e-02 | 3.5e-02 / 1.8e-01 | 7.6e-03 / 4.6e-02 | 2.2e-03 / 1.2e-02 | 5.0e-04 / 2.8e-03 |
| GRE, 117° RF + gradient spoiling | 95% | 8.7e-01 / 9.5e-01 | 1.9e-01 / 8.4e-01 | 9.8e-02 / 6.1e-01 | 4.8e-02 / 3.6e-01 | 2.4e-02 / 1.8e-01 |

Entries: max rel. k-space difference / image NRMSE; the second column is the relative L2 k-space difference between MRzero's own box and point voxels (how much the voxel model matters).

On the EPI, where a voxel never holds more than half a cycle of phase, the difference falls as ~K⁻² (stratified jitter on a smooth integrand); 1×1 is the voxel centre, which beats one random point per voxel. On the spoiled GRE the PDG holds states that have wound up to ~80 cycles across a voxel after 40 TRs: MRzero integrates them analytically, while K×K points resolve them only once K exceeds the cycles per voxel, so the difference falls as ~1/K. This is the guidance for sub-voxel isochromats: their number per voxel side must exceed the largest dephasing (in cycles per voxel) that still carries signal.
