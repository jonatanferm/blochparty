How the model works

Anatomy. BrainWeb gives, for each 0.5 mm voxel, the fraction of each of 12 tissue classes (CSF, grey and white matter, fat, muscle, skin, skull, marrow, vessels, dura, connective tissue, air). The fractions are resampled to the simulation grid by exact volume averaging, so partial volume survives.

Spins. Every voxel becomes spins of each tissue it contains, at jittered sub-voxel positions, each weighted by that tissue's fraction and carrying its own T1, T2 and proton density from a cited table (3 T: Stanisz, Spijkerman, de Bazelaire and others), optionally with within-tissue texture, magnetization transfer and diffusion. Tissues are never averaged into one voxel value.

Field. Each tissue has a magnetic susceptibility (water −9.05 ppm, air +0.36, bone about −2 relative to water). The B0 field of the whole head is computed by Fourier dipole convolution, then a second-order shim is fitted over the brain, as a scanner would. Each spin sees the field where it sits.

Surfaces here are 50 % iso-surfaces of the fractions at 1.5 mm, for display only; the simulation uses the fractions themselves.
